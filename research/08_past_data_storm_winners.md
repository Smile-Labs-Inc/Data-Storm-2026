- Data Storm rewards more than leaderboard score: official copy repeats Think, Code, Analyze, Pitch, and past rounds used written reports, viva, business pitch, and finalist mentoring. Use this as a business case, not only a model contest. Sources: https://datastorm.rotaract.social/ , https://chokolaate.net/data-storm-2-0-sri-lankas-premier-advanced-analytics-competition/ , https://blog.rotaractmora.org/down-the-memory-lane/

- For this outlet-potential task, the strongest story is censored or latent demand: observed volume is not the same as maximum possible volume. Pair this with geospatial/POI features, leakage-safe validation, and a short financial-impact narrative. ## Comparable Kaggle and Retail Demand Work

- Rossmann Store Sales is a useful analogue for store-level volume prediction: the 2nd-place winner said he spent over 70% of time on feature engineering, found signal in rows many people removed, and used XGBoost plus moving-average and calendar/promotion features. Source: https://medium.com/kaggle-blog/rossmann-store-sales-winners-interview-2nd-place-nima-shahbazi-ad7a4eb65629
- Favorita Grocery Sales is a relevant grocery/FMCG forecasting competition, but the fetched Kaggle writeup page exposed only the title and no usable solution details. Source: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/favorita-grocery-sales-forecasting/writeups/w-1st-place-solution
- Censored-demand literature is directly relevant: observed sales can understate true demand when inventory or availability limits sales. Sources: https://juanitorduz.github.io/demand/ , https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16319v2
- FreshRetailNet-50K frames latent demand recovery as a two-stage problem: reconstruct demand during stockouts, then forecast on recovered demand; the paper reports lower underestimation bias after recovery. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16319v2
- Tobit and censored-likelihood approaches are relevant when the target is bounded or censored by observed supply, not pure demand. Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2409.05412v1 , https://juanitorduz.github.io/demand/

# Winner Patterns

- Winners likely connect model output to business action. Data Storm 1.0 ended with pitches on how to solve a business challenge, Data Storm 2.0 used a viva and business pitch, and Data Storm 3.0 required feasible scenario solutions. Sources: https://www.keells.lk/posts/octave-sponsors-data-storm-1-0 , https://chokolaate.net/data-storm-2-0-sri-lankas-premier-advanced-analytics-competition/ , https://blog.rotaractmora.org/down-the-memory-lane/
- Strong teams show clean EDA, data preparation, and reporting. Data Storm 2.0 Masterclass 1.0 covered data processing, EDA, visualization, classification, and evaluation, and teams submitted reports. Source: https://chokolaate.net/data-storm-2-0-sri-lankas-premier-advanced-analytics-competition/
- Winning work is explainable enough for viva/Q&A. Data Storm 2.0 used a Viva Round before final presentations, and Data Storm 3.0 finalists had mentoring before the finale. Sources: https://chokolaate.net/data-storm-2-0-sri-lankas-premier-advanced-analytics-competition/ , https://blog.rotaractmora.org/down-the-memory-lane/
- The judge-facing story should include financial impact or KPI logic. OCTAVE's own methodology says analytics value flows through KPI improvement and should be measured via test/control, pre/post, or hybrid setups when possible. Source: https://medium.com/octave-john-keells-group/measuring-financial-impact-of-analytics-use-cases-9308915f666b
- Practical validation matters. OCTAVE's delivery article stresses acceptance criteria, testing criteria, risks, dependencies, business show-and-tell, and KPI tracking before delivery. Source: https://medium.com/octave-john-keells-group/end-to-end-analytics-use-case-delivery-the-big-picture-5bc78f60193c
- Feature engineering beats blind tuning in comparable retail Kaggle work. The Rossmann 2nd-place winner said he spent more than 70% of effort on feature engineering and discovered value in rows that others removed. Source: https://medium.com/kaggle-blog/rossmann-store-sales-winners-interview-2nd-place-nima-shahbazi-ad7a4eb65629
- Strong local hackathon projects show a working prototype or practical plan, not just a notebook. HackElite and IDEALIZE both emphasize proposal/prototype/final pitch flows. Sources: https://hackelite.ieeewieuom.lk/ , https://idealize.aiesec.lk/

# Loser Patterns

- Treating the task as only a leaderboard problem is risky. Data Storm public pages repeatedly include reports, viva, mentoring, and pitches, so a pure model score without business reasoning leaves 60% of the current rubric exposed. Sources: https://datastorm.rotaract.social/ , https://chokolaate.net/data-storm-2-0-sri-lankas-premier-advanced-analytics-competition/ , https://blog.rotaractmora.org/down-the-memory-lane/
- Ignoring censored demand is risky for outlet potential. Retail demand papers warn that sales data can systematically underestimate true demand when availability or stockouts cap observed sales. Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16319v2 , https://juanitorduz.github.io/demand/
- Throwing away "weird" rows can lose signal. The Rossmann winner interview says many competitors removed zero-sales rows, while the 2nd-place solution extracted useful holiday/closure features from them. Source: https://medium.com/kaggle-blog/rossmann-store-sales-winners-interview-2nd-place-nima-shahbazi-ad7a4eb65629
- Weak target definition is risky. OCTAVE's churn article spends most of its effort defining the target before modeling, because wrong target definitions create wrong interventions. Source: https://medium.com/octave-john-keells-group/a-simple-six-step-approach-to-define-customer-churn-in-retail-f401e31e57c0
- Failing to separate data issues from algorithm issues is risky. OCTAVE's delivery article explicitly lists data issues, platform issues, algorithm issues, and parameter issues as impediments to track. Source: https://medium.com/octave-john-keells-group/end-to-end-analytics-use-case-delivery-the-big-picture-5bc78f60193c
- A report that has no measured impact logic is weak. OCTAVE says analytics impact should be tied to KPI improvement and annualized financial impact where possible. Source: https://medium.com/octave-john-keells-group/measuring-financial-impact-of-analytics-use-cases-9308915f666b
- Overclaiming public history is risky. Public winner data for Data Storm 4.0 to 6.0 was not found in reliable, fetchable writeups during this search, so claiming lineage without proof may hurt credibility. Sources: https://datastorm.rotaract.social/ , https://datastorm.rotaract.social/webinars/webinar_4.html , https://datastorm.rotaract.social/webinars/webinar_5.html

# 7 Takeaways for THIS Team

1. Lead with the latent-demand frame: "observed monthly volume is a lower-bound or noisy proxy for maximum outlet potential." Use censored-demand language and show why naive supervised learning can under-predict constrained outlets. Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16319v2 , https://juanitorduz.github.io/demand/
2. Build a two-layer solution: first estimate outlet opportunity or uncapped potential features, then train/ensemble robust tabular models. This maps cleanly to FreshRetailNet's recover-then-forecast framing, even if your data is cross-sectional instead of hourly stockout data. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16319v2
3. Spend report space on forensics. Data Storm 2.0 judged reports and model effectiveness, and the current rubric gives 40% to DE/forensics; show missingness, impossible coordinates, duplicates, outliers, leakage checks, target distribution, and feature sanity. Sources: https://chokolaate.net/data-storm-2-0-sri-lankas-premier-advanced-analytics-competition/ , https://datastorm.rotaract.social/
4. Use POI/geospatial features only with a clear causal story. For beverage outlets, useful stories are foot traffic, affluence, urban density, tourist demand, school/office clusters, competition, road access, and outlet type; tie each group to expected maximum volume. This aligns with Data Storm's emphasis on feasible scenario solutions. Source: https://blog.rotaractmora.org/down-the-memory-lane/
5. Prepare a 10-minute business pitch before finalizing the notebook. Data Storm 1.0, 2.0, and 3.0 all had pitch or presentation components, so the team needs a simple story: what drives potential, what actions the distributor should take, and how to measure impact. Sources: https://www.keells.lk/posts/octave-sponsors-data-storm-1-0 , https://chokolaate.net/data-storm-2-0-sri-lankas-premier-advanced-analytics-competition/ , https://blog.rotaractmora.org/down-the-memory-lane/
6. Include a KPI and impact measurement section. Use OCTAVE's own framing: outlet uplift should be evaluated through comparable groups, pre/post, or a pilot/control design, then translated into revenue or margin impact. Source: https://medium.com/octave-john-keells-group/measuring-financial-impact-of-analytics-use-cases-9308915f666b
7. Do not hide uncertainty. Public Data Storm history is sparse and the target is latent, so state assumptions, caveats, and model limits plainly; this is more credible than pretending exact true potential is directly observed. Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16319v2 , https://medium.com/octave-john-keells-group/end-to-end-analytics-use-case-delivery-the-big-picture-5bc78f60193c
